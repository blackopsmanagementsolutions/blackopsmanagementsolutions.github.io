The person who never asks

The help already exists. The connection does not.

A university, a company or a hospital already has people whose job is to help. Each usually runs as its own silo, and there is no single human point of contact. The person who is struggling meets a list, not a person.

  1. 01They do not know there is a problemNobody puts their hand up to say they are struggling. Often a person does not know that what they are carrying has a name, or that anything can be done.
  2. 02It is already too muchA struggling person has to work out for themselves who to go to.
  3. 03They are handed a listNames, departments and phone numbers. Unfamiliar places, and no idea which one is right.
  4. 04Nobody is expecting themThe silos do not know they are coming, or why.
  5. 05So they go nowhereInaction is the default, and the most common outcome.

Unseen.Unheard.Disconnected.Unsupported.

Why now

Indian institutions must now do more than appoint a counsellor.

Fifteen guidelines, binding on every school, college and university in India until Parliament legislates. They ask for counsellors, referral links, written escalation, staff training twice a year, sessions for parents, and a yearly report to the regulator. The University Grants Commission followed with its own policy.

All educational institutions shall establish written protocols for immediate referral to mental health services, local hospitals, and suicide prevention helplines. Supreme Court of India, 25 July 2025Guideline V of fifteen. Sukdeb Saha v. State of Andhra Pradesh.
Failure to take timely or adequate action in such cases, especially where such neglect contributes to a student’s self-harm or suicide, shall be treated as institutional culpability. Guideline VIIIOn how an institution handles complaints of harassment, ragging, bullying and assault.

What a deployment includes

A room, the people in it, and everything that forms around a person who walks in.

A Lythous™ node is a staffed room on your site. Anyone you are responsible for can walk in without an appointment and without telling anyone why. Behind the room stand trained people, a written protocol for the person at risk, and a network of independent professionals. All of it held to CARE™.

AcknowledgedAmbassadors

The first point of human contact. Trained to notice the person who is always alone, or who stopped turning up, and to walk with them rather than point at a door. Drawn from your own students, staff and community.

ConnectedListeners

The first human a person reaches by phone, text or WhatsApp, at any hour. A person, not a menu. Somebody who is frightened at three in the morning should reach a human being.

RespectedThe network

Independent, credentialled professionals across India, each practising in their own right. A node connects a person to them and stays with the person until they have arrived. It does not deliver care itself.

Five steps, and somebody responsible for every one.

What happens after a person makes contact. The sequence is the same everywhere; what forms around the person is not.

  1. 01
    AcknowledgedRecognition

    The person reaches out, or an Ambassador notices them. Either way, it starts with a person, not a form.

  2. 02
    ConnectedEngagement

    They talk to a Listener, on their terms. In the room, or by phone, text or WhatsApp.

  3. 03
    RespectedNavigation

    Together they work out what would actually help, and who is the right resource, starting with the ones the institution already has.

  4. 04
    ConnectedEscalation

    If necessary, the person is connected with the right person or resource. A written protocol names who acts for anyone at risk, in what order, and how fast.

  5. 05
    EmpoweredFollow-through

    We do not just show the next door. We stay with the person until they get through it, and somebody checks back afterwards.

What a deployment covers, against what the guidelines ask for.

A map, line by line, with the source for every claim. Not a compliance assessment, and not legal advice.

  • A written wellbeing policy, published and reviewed each yearGuideline IRespectedA deployment supplies the policy document and the annual review cycle.
  • Formal referral linkages with external professionalsGuideline IIConnectedThe network of eleven independent professionals, each credentialled and practising in their own right, under a formal arrangement.
  • Consistent, informal and confidential support for small groups of studentsGuideline IIIAcknowledgedAmbassadors and Listeners. The informal layer is written into the guideline itself.
  • Written protocols for immediate referral, prominently displayedGuideline VConnectedThe escalation protocol: who acts, in what order, how fast. Posted in the node and known to every Ambassador.
  • All staff trained twice a year by certified professionalsGuideline VIEmpoweredAmbassador and staff training, run on a twice-yearly cycle with the network’s professionals.
  • Regular sensitisation sessions for parents and guardiansGuideline IXConnectedSessions for families, from the same room and the same people.
  • Anonymised records and an annual report to the regulatorGuideline XEmpoweredFigures with no names attached: how many came, how many were connected onward, how many arrived. Once a year, a report you can put in front of a regulator.

Nobody who uses a node becomes a patient. When someone needs professional care, they are connected to an independent qualified professional, and from that point the relationship is with them. Whether a deployment satisfies any guideline is a judgement for your own counsel.
