02 / The problem
The missing layer

The help already exists. The connection doesn’t.

Universities already have people on campus whose job is to help. But each area typically runs as its own silo, and there is no central point of contact.

  • Doctors
  • Counsellors
  • Hospitals
  • Faculty
  • Student services
  • Technology

Unseen.Unheard.Disconnected.Unsupported.

An overwhelmed student often does not know where to go, or who to turn to.Here is how they experience it

  1. 01 They do not know there is a problem Nobody puts their hand up to say they are broken. Often a person does not know that what they are carrying has a name, or that anything can be done.
  2. 02 It is already too much A struggling individual has to figure out who to go to with their issues.
  3. 03 They are often handed a list Names, departments and phone numbers. Unfamiliar places, and they have no idea which one is correct.
  4. 04 Nobody is expecting them These silos do not know they are coming, or what their issues are.
  5. 05 So they go nowhere Inaction is the default, and it is the most common outcome.

Many times, the first time anyone hears of a person in crisis is after something has gone badly wrong…

03 / Why now
Why now

Indian universities must now do more than hire a counsellor.

In the court’s own words
All educational institutions shall establish written protocols for immediate referral to mental health services, local hospitals, and suicide prevention helplines. Supreme Court of India, 25 July 2025Guideline V of fifteen. Sukdeb Saha v. State of Andhra Pradesh.
Failure to take timely or adequate action in such cases, especially where such neglect contributes to a student’s self-harm or suicide, shall be treated as institutional culpability, making the administration liable to regulatory and legal consequences. Guideline VIIIOn how an institution handles complaints of harassment, ragging, bullying and assault.

Fifteen guidelines, binding on every school, college and university in India until Parliament legislates. They ask for counsellors, referral links, written escalation, staff training twice a year, sessions for parents, and a yearly report to your regulator. The University Grants Commission added its own policy on 13 January 2026.

We show you what a node covers. Your legal team decides what it satisfies. This is not legal advice, and anyone who promises you compliance is overselling.

04 / The possibility
The possibility

What becomes possible when we understand more of the person?

Science, medicine and technology give us remarkable abilities. Their greatest promise may not be what they can do for us, but what they help us understand about ourselves and what we can become.

Human Infrastructure™ is the connecting layer around a person. It brings together human connection, understanding, care, technology, resources and follow-through. Not only when somebody becomes ill. Not only when somebody reaches crisis. Throughout a life.

Lythous™ begins with the person.

Instead of beginning with “which of our services do you need?”, we begin with:

  • Who are you?
  • What is happening in your life?
  • What matters to you?
  • What might help you move forward?

Then we try to understand more of the person, so the right people, care and resources can be organised around what they actually need.

08 / How it works
How it works

Five steps, from noticing someone who feels unseen and unheard. An Ambassador is responsible for each one.

  1. Listen
  2. Understand
  3. Recognise
  4. Connect
  5. Care
  6. Follow through
  7. Become
01

Recognition

Some people reach out themselves. Others are invited by a trained Ambassador, who notices what a timetable misses: the student who is always alone, or the one who stopped turning up.

02

Engagement

A conversation on the person’s terms, in the room or by phone, text or WhatsApp.

03

Navigation

We work out what would help, and which of the university’s existing resources fits.

04

Escalation

A written plan covers anyone at risk: it names who is contacted, in what order, and how fast.

05

Follow-through

We go with the person until they have arrived and confirm that they did, and afterwards somebody checks back with them.

12 / What changes
What changes

What is different a year after a node opens.

People come forward who never would have. Not the students who already book appointments, but the ones who were never going to.

The services you already pay for get used, and used earlier. A node does not add another service. It makes the ones you have easier to reach, and reaches people while it is still a conversation.

You can see whether it is working. You get figures with no names attached: how many people came, how many were connected onward, and how many arrived. Once a year there is a report you can put in front of a regulator, and it never carries anybody’s name.

What leadership sees. How much the room is used, how people engage, and whether connections are followed through, all reported without names. Enough to act on, and nothing that identifies a person.

Who we serve

Universities and colleges

“How do we support the thousands of students who never walk into counselling?”

The reality

Most institutions already have counsellors, doctors, wardens and student services. A struggling student never meets any of it as one system. They meet a list of places they have never been, and none of those places knows they are coming.

What a node adds

  • A staffed room anyone can walk into. No appointment, no referral letter
  • Trained Ambassadors whose job is to notice the student who never asks
  • A written protocol for urgent cases: who is contacted, in what order, how quickly
  • A formal referral route to independent qualified professionals
  • Somebody who stays with the student until they have arrived, and checks back afterwards

What leadership sees

Anonymised numbers on use, engagement and follow-through, plus an annual report. Never individual names.

Who we serve

Hospitals and healthcare

“What happens to a person between appointments?”

The reality

The gap is rarely clinical capacity. It is the weeks between appointments, when nobody is responsible for the person and nobody notices that they stopped coming.

What a node adds

  • A non-clinical layer around the clinical one
  • Someone who spots trouble early, helps the person find the right service, and follows through between appointments
  • A person who checks that the handoff actually happened

A node delivers no care and sits outside your clinical governance. Referral goes to independent qualified professionals, and from that point the relationship is with them.

For professionals

Where you fit

“Where do I fit, and does my practice stay mine?”

You practise in your own right. A Lythous™ node refers people to you. It does not employ you to deliver care, and it does not deliver care itself.

What that means in practice

  • From the point of referral, the relationship is between you and the person
  • You keep your credentials, your standards and your record keeping
  • Referrals arrive with context, not as a name and a number
