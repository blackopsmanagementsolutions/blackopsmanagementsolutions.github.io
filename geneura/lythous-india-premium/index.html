The problem

Your university already has the resources. The missing part is the connection between them.

Doctors, counsellors, hospitals, faculty, student services and technology. Each one holds a part of the picture.

A student who is struggling meets none of it as a system. They meet a list of places they have never been, none of which knows they are coming. So a struggling student can end up going nowhere at all, and the first time anyone finds out is when something has already broken.

In plain terms

We organise what we offer around the person, not the other way round.

A Lythous is a room on your campus with people in it. Students, faculty and families can all walk in. You do not need an appointment or a referral letter, and there is no waiting list.

If someone needs more than a conversation, we find them the right professional and go with them until they have actually arrived. Nobody is given a phone number and left to it.

A Lythous is not a clinic or a counselling service. Nobody who walks in becomes a patient. When professional care is needed we refer to an independent qualified professional, and from that point the relationship is with them.

What that means for a student

You don’t have to know what’s wrong, or call it anything, or have a reason that sounds serious enough.

You can just come in.

People reach us by walking in, phoning, texting, WhatsApp or email, whichever is easiest at the time.

Inside a Lythous

Built to feel like somewhere you’d actually sit down.

There is no desk between two people, and nothing in the room that makes you feel you are being assessed.

Who it reaches

A campus is more than its students.

The same room and the same people, for everyone the institution is responsible for.

Students

A student can come in without a referral or a diagnosis, including the ones who would never book an appointment.

Faculty & staff

How it works

Seven steps, and somebody owns every one of them.

Recognition

Trained Ambassadors on campus notice what a timetable misses, like the student who is always alone or the one who stopped arriving on time.

Engagement

We start with a conversation on the person’s terms, in the room or by phone, text or WhatsApp.

Navigation

We work out what would help, and which of the university’s existing resources fits.

Professional care

We refer to an independent qualified professional where that is needed. From then on the relationship is with them.

Escalation

A written protocol covers anyone at risk. It names who is contacted, in what order, and how quickly.

Referral

We stay with the person until they have actually arrived, and we check that they did.

Follow-through

Someone checks back afterwards, and records that they did.

Why now

Indian institutions are being asked to do more than appoint a counsellor.

Recent Supreme Court and UGC guidance sets out what higher-education institutions are expected to have in place. That covers a mental health policy, qualified support or formal referral linkages, written escalation protocols, staff training, parent sensitisation and anonymised annual reporting to the regulator.

Most universities already hold pieces of that. What’s usually missing is the layer that makes them operate as one system around a student.

We show you what a deployment covers. Your counsel decides what it satisfies. We will go through it line by line against each obligation, with the source for every claim. Anyone promising you compliance is overselling.
