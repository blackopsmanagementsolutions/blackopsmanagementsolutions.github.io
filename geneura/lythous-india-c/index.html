Monday, 9.40am

On the surface everything looked fine. She showed up to class. She smiled when people looked. She told her parents she was fine.

The same week

Inside, it was classes, expectations, loneliness, and the feeling that everyone else was managing better than she was.

Every day after that
Keep going.It became harder.Keep going.It became harder.

Nobody noticed the change. Not because people did not care, but because nobody knew what to look for.

Somewhere in there

She did not want to be labelled. She did not want a file, or a diagnosis, or a conversation that started with what is wrong with you.

She wanted one person to understand that something was not right.

One night

She almost gave up. Not because she was weak. Because she felt invisible.

Now imagine

A different ending.

Imagine somebody had seen it earlier. Not a system. A person, in a room she already walked past every day.

The week before

An Ambassador notices she has stopped arriving on time, and that she sits alone in a room she used to sit in with other people. Nothing dramatic. Just noticed.

That afternoon

Someone says hello properly. There is no form and no assessment. She does not have to know what is wrong, or call it anything, or have a reason that sounds serious enough. She can just come in.

Within the week

What she needed turned out to be something the university already had. She had never been told it existed, and would not have known how to ask.

So somebody walked her there. Not a phone number. A person, who stayed until she had actually arrived.

A month later

Someone checked back. That is the part every other system drops.

The point

Mary is not a rare case. She is the gap the systems were never built to hold.

Mary is a composite, drawn from the situations Lythous is built for. She is an illustration, not a case study.

The room she walked into

A place, not a portal.

Daylight, low seating, and nothing in the room that makes you feel you are being assessed. There is no desk between two people.

Not a clinic and not a counselling service. When professional care is needed we refer to an independent qualified professional, and from that point the relationship is with them.

Why now

Institutions are being asked to do more than appoint a counsellor.

Recent Supreme Court and UGC guidance sets out what higher-education institutions are expected to have in place: a mental health policy, qualified support or formal referral linkages, written escalation protocols, staff training, parent sensitisation and anonymised annual reporting.
