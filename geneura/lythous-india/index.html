Who on your campus isn’t being seen?

A Lythous is a staffed room inside your university. Students, faculty and families walk in and talk to a real person — and we connect them to the doctors, counsellors and services you already have.

The problem

The resources already exist. The connection between them often doesn’t.

Your university already has doctors, counsellors, hospitals, faculty, student services and technology. Each one holds a part of the picture.

A student who is struggling meets none of it as a system. They meet a list of places they have never been, none of which knows they are coming. So most of them go nowhere at all, and the first time anyone finds out is when something has already broken.

In plain terms

We organise what we offer around the person, not the other way round.

A Lythous is a room on your campus with people in it. Anyone can walk in — students, faculty, families. No appointment, no referral letter, no waiting list.

If someone needs more than a conversation, we find them the right professional and stay with them until they actually get there. Warm handoffs, not cold referrals.

It isn’t a clinic and it isn’t a counselling service. Nobody who walks into a Lythous becomes a patient. When professional care is needed we refer to an independent qualified professional, and from that point the relationship is with them.

What that means for a student

You don’t have to know what’s wrong. You don’t have to call it anything. You don’t need a reason that sounds serious enough.

You can just come in.

Reachable by walk-in, phone, text, WhatsApp or email — whichever is easiest at the time.

Inside a Lythous

Built to feel like somewhere you’d actually sit down.

No desk between two people. No waiting-room chairs. Nowhere that feels like being assessed.

Who it reaches

A campus is not only its students.

The same room, the same people, the same front door — for everyone the institution is responsible for.

Students

Reachable without a referral, a diagnosis, or a reason that sounds serious enough — including the ones who would never book an appointment.

Faculty & staff

Confidential support for the people carrying the institution, and training so they know what to do when a student comes to them first.

Families

Sensitisation for parents and guardians, and somewhere to turn when a family is carrying more than anyone outside it can see.

How it works

Seven steps, and somebody owns every one of them.

01

Recognition

Trained Ambassadors on campus notice what timetables don’t — the student always alone, the one who stopped arriving on time.

02

Engagement

A first conversation, on the person’s terms. In the room, or by phone, text or WhatsApp.

03

Navigation

Working out what would actually help, and which of the university’s existing resources fits.

04

Professional care

Referral to an independent qualified professional where it’s needed. That relationship is theirs, not ours.

05

Escalation

A written protocol for anyone at risk. Who is contacted, in what order, how fast.

06

Referral

A closed loop. We stay with the person until they have actually arrived.

07

Follow-through

Someone checks back afterwards. This is the step every other system drops.

Why now

Indian institutions are being asked to do more than appoint a counsellor.

Recent Supreme Court and UGC guidance sets out what higher-education institutions are expected to have in place — a mental health policy, qualified support or formal referral linkages, written escalation protocols, staff training, parent sensitisation, and anonymised annual reporting to the regulator.

Most universities already hold pieces of that. What’s usually missing is the layer that makes them operate as one system around a student.

We map, we don’t guarantee. We’ll show you line by line what a deployment provides against each obligation, with the source for every claim. Your counsel confirms applicability. Anyone promising you compliance is overselling.
