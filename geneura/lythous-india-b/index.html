01 / The gap

Not a clinic. Not an app. Not a single service.

Universities already have doctors, counsellors, hospitals, faculty and student services. Each holds a part of the picture.

A struggling student meets none of it as a system. They meet a list of places they have never been, none of which knows they are coming. So they go nowhere, and the first anyone hears of it is when something has already broken.

Lythous is the layer that connects what an institution already has, around one person, so that no student, faculty member or family ever feels unseen, unheard or unsupported.

The boundary

Nobody who walks into a Lythous becomes a patient. When professional care is needed we refer to an independent qualified professional, and from that point the relationship is with them.

03 / What it provides

One room, and everyone the institution is responsible for.

For students

24/7 access to a real person, with no referral, diagnosis or waiting list. Personalised pathways, and follow-up that continues after the first conversation.

For faculty and staff

Confidential support of their own, and preventative work against stress and burnout, plus training for when a student comes to them first.

For parents and families

Early recognition before things escalate, continuity of care, and somewhere to turn when a family is carrying more than anyone outside it can see.

Youth Ambassador Programme

Leadership recognition, stipends and structured growth pathways. Students become the people who notice, and gain something real for it.

Internships

Hands-on placements across healthcare, wellness and operations, with mentorship. Designed to create opportunity, not just access.

The provider network

Independent qualified professionals, practising in their own right, reachable through a formal referral pathway rather than a phone number.

04 / Inside

07 / The mandate

Institutions are being asked to do more than appoint a counsellor.

Recent Supreme Court and UGC guidance sets out what higher-education institutions are expected to have in place: a mental health policy, qualified support or formal referral linkages, written escalation protocols, staff training, parent sensitisation and anonymised annual reporting.

Most universities already hold pieces of that. What is usually missing is the layer that makes them work as one system around a student.

How we talk about compliance

We show you what a deployment covers. Your counsel decides what it satisfies. Line by line against each obligation, with the source for every claim.

Anyone promising you compliance is overselling.
